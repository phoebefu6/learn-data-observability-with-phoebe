Learn Data Observability with Phoebe

Pipelines that never fail still ship wrong numbers. A shard stops arriving and the row count is at 41 percent of a normal day's rows for two business days; a merge key changes and the duplicate rate triples while the count looks perfect; a column disappears and two dashboards quietly lose a filter. None of that raises an error, so the person who finds it is a stakeholder. This course builds the layer that finds it first: four cheap metadata queries, thresholds learned from a table's own history rather than guessed, and a bench that scores your monitor set over 30 nights of real load metadata with five real defects in it. Six leader sessions on what to fund and what to report; eight builder sessions that end with a monitor set you have actually measured.

What is real here 🧾

A course about detecting quiet failures should be exact about which of its own numbers are measured.

ClaimStatus
The 30 nights of load metadataGenerated once from a fixed seed and identical for every learner. Row counts, load lateness, duplicate rates, null rates and column sets, with a real weekday and weekend pattern
The five defectsInjected into specific nights: a 230-minute late load, a load holding 41 percent of a normal count, a duplicate rate of 8.4 percent, a dropped column, and a null rate of 11.4 percent. On a defect night every other signal is held at a quiet baseline, so credit for a catch can only go to the monitor watching that pillar
The ladder: 0, 1, 2, 2, 3, 4, 5 of 5 caughtComputed by the bench in your browser, night by night. Trailing percentiles, same-weekday medians, duplicate rates, column-set diffs and standard deviations are all real arithmetic on that history
Eight false pages from a fixed volume bandArithmetic. Weekend volume is about 45 percent of a weekday, which sits outside a plus-or-minus 30 percent band on all eight weekend nights in the window
Tightening everything: 2 of 5, 25 false pages, 15.8 hoursThe counts and the thresholds are real. The consequence runs through one modelled rule, below
The muted channelModelled, not measured. Six or more false pages in a trailing week is treated as a channel nobody reads, so the page fires and the stakeholder still finds the problem first. Labelled as a model on the widget itself
Great Expectations component namesRead from the GX Core 1.x documentation: Data Context, Data Source, Data Asset, Batch Definition, Expectation, Expectation Suite, Validation Definition, Checkpoint, Actions, Data Docs
DAMA-DMBOK's six dimensions and ISO/IEC 25012Both named as published, including the point that uniqueness appears in DAMA and not in the ISO inherent set. The ISO standard itself is paywalled, so wording travels through close secondary sources and is flagged where it does
BellwetherA fictional grocery delivery company. The failure patterns are ordinary ones; the company is not real
